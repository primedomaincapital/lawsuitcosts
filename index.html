The Thesis

Every industry prices risk.
Litigation still prices blind.

Insurance has its actuarial tables. Credit has its bureaus. Real estate has its comps. Yet litigation — a $400 billion annual expenditure in the United States alone — still prices itself case by case, partner by partner, on instinct and anecdote. The data exists, scattered across dockets, invoices, and settlement ledgers. What has never existed is the name under which it all gets assembled.

LawsuitCosts.com is that name. Two plain words, in the exact order a general counsel, a claims officer, or a litigation funder types them into a search bar. It carries the authority of a public record and the directness of a line item — a brand that reads like an institution on day one.

The litigation finance market has grown past $15 billion globally, and every fund in it underwrites on one question: what will this case cost, and what is it worth? The platforms that answer that question — benchmarking tools, spend analytics, outcome databases — need a home that says exactly what they do. This domain is the rare asset that does the positioning work before the first slide of the pitch deck.

What a lawsuit actually costs

Litigation cost benchmarks drawn from court statistics, corporate counsel surveys, and published filing-fee schedules.

Ask a room of lawyers what a lawsuit costs and the answers scatter from five figures to eight. The public record is less dramatic and more useful: federal courts now decide well under 1% of civil cases at trial, most state civil judgments are small, and corporate litigation budgets, after climbing for years, eased in 2025. The figures below keep those three facts in the same frame, because the price of suing is set less by the courtroom than by everything that happens before anyone gets there.

How to read these numbers

Included: federal trial-rate data, the Norton Rose Fulbright corporate counsel survey, National Center for State Courts caseload studies, and a ten-state filing-fee survey. Excluded: hourly billing-rate guides, settlement medians from private databases, and any paywalled litigation-cost table. Where a source publishes a prior-period figure, it is shown beside the current one; where it does not, the row is marked Baseline rather than given an invented comparison.

Disclaimer: Litigation costs vary sharply by case type, jurisdiction, and how far a case proceeds, and a filing fee is only the first line of a much longer bill. Figures on this page are aggregated public estimates assembled for information only.
